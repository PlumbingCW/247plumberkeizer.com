Water heater installation, done on your schedule

Installing a water heater is more than setting a tank on a stand. The unit has to be sized to your household, connected to water and fuel, vented if gas, and fitted with a relief valve and discharge pipe. It also has to meet the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state plumbing code that sets those requirements.

This page is for planned installs: new construction, a remodel, a fuel change or an upgrade before the old tank fails. If your heater has already failed, see water heater replacement for same-day help.

Choosing the type

Each type has a place. We help you pick before ordering anything.

Standard gas tank

The usual choice for homes with natural gas. Needs venting and combustion air.

Standard electric tank

Simple to install, no venting, widely available.

Heat pump water heater

Pulls heat from the surrounding air and uses far less electricity. Needs space and a drain.

Tankless

Heats on demand and saves floor space. Needs venting and proper gas sizing. See tankless water heaters.

What happens on installation day

A planned install runs on a schedule we agree on in advance.

1

Prepare

The unit is set, connected with new supply lines, a relief valve and discharge pipe, vented if gas, and fitted with an expansion tank when needed.

3

Permits and inspection

The City of Keizer says permits are required for plumbing alterations and additions and are issued to properly licensed and bonded contractors. The Building Department is at 930 Chemawa Road NE and the phone is (503) 856-3440. Permit rules vary by project, so ask the department whether a like-for-like swap or a fuel change needs one.

You can verify any plumber’s license with the Oregon Building Codes Division license search, and plumbers must also work under a Construction Contractors Board license. It takes a few minutes and is worth doing before work starts.

Keizer notes

Installing in a Keizer home

The City of Keizer reports development began in earnest in the 1950s and grew through the 1970s. Houses from those years often have the heater in a garage or utility closet, with room for a like-for-like unit. A heat pump unit needs more space and a drain, so we measure before recommending one.

If your project is in Woodburn, the city says Marion County issues plumbing permits there. In Silverton and Salem, the local building offices handle them. We check the right office for your address.
