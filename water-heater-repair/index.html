When a leaking or dead water heater is urgent

A water heater that is leaking from the tank, or one that has no hot water on a cold morning, cannot wait for next week. Our 24/7 dispatch covers nights and weekends. Before we arrive, shut the cold water supply valve on top of the heater and switch the unit off at the breaker or the gas control.

Do not touch a tank that is hissing or shows steam from the relief valve pipe, and keep children away from any pooled water near the electrical panel.

  • ✓
    Shut the cold supply valve. It sits on the pipe feeding the top of the tank. Turning it stops new water from entering a leaking tank.
  • ✓
    Turn the power or gas off. Use the breaker for an electric unit or the gas control knob for a gas unit.
  • ✓
    Open one hot tap upstairs. It relieves pressure while the tank is isolated. Leave it open until we arrive.
  • ✓
    Protect what is nearby. Move boxes and stored items away from the base of the heater and lay towels to slow the spread.

What usually goes wrong with a water heater

Most repair calls come down to a short list of parts. Knowing which one is failing tells you whether to fix or replace.

No hot water

On an electric tank it is often a failed heating element or thermostat. On a gas tank it is usually the pilot, thermocouple or gas valve.

Lukewarm water

A burned out lower element, sediment around the burner or a dip tube problem leaves the tank only partly hot.

Popping or rumbling

Sediment at the bottom of the tank traps steam bubbles. A flush often quiets it if the tank is sound.

Water at the base

A loose fitting or a drain valve drip is repairable. A leak from the tank shell itself is not.

Repair or replace: how the decision gets made

A leak from a fitting, a relief valve or a drain valve is a repair. A leak through the steel tank wall is the end of that tank, because the liner cannot be patched. When the tank is intact, the plumber checks age, anode condition and parts cost against the price of a new unit.

As a rule, a heater past its early years that needs a major part is better replaced, and our plumber will say so plainly. If that is where you land, water heater replacement covers the sizing, the work and the cost factors.

Hybrid units are electric heat pump water heaters. A heat pump unit also needs clear space and a drain for condensate.

Sediment, anode rods and flushing

Sediment settles at the bottom of every tank. Left alone it insulates the water from the burner or element, shortens tank life and makes noise. A flush clears it, and the anode rod, which protects the steel, is checked at the same visit.

The anode rod is the part that sacrifices itself so the tank does not rust. When it is used up, the tank starts to corrode. Replacing the rod early extends the tank, but only if the tank is still sound.

Ask your water utility for its annual water quality report if you want to know what is in your supply, or test your tap. The City of Keizer publishes an annual water report for its groundwater system.

Standards behind a safe water heater repair

A water heater must have a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe. That requirement comes from the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and is carried into the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code. A valve that drips or a discharge pipe that was capped off is a safety problem, not a nuisance.

Electric units are wired under the National Electrical Code (NEC), so any wiring change beyond a thermostat or element swap belongs with a licensed electrician. Gas valves, burners and vent parts follow the manufacturer’s service instructions. A repair that touches gas piping or venting may need a permit, so ask before work starts.
