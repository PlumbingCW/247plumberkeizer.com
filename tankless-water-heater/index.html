How a tankless water heater works

A tankless heater has no storage tank. When you open a hot tap, water flows through a heat exchanger and a burner or element heats it to the set temperature. It stops heating when you close the tap.

That saves the standby heat a tank loses all day, but it also means the unit must deliver a lot of heat quickly. The gas line, the venting and the flow rate all have to match the unit, or it will cycle, fault or shut off.

Where tankless works best and where it struggles

The fit depends on your household.

Small households

One shower or sink

A unit sized for a single use works, but flow rate and minimum flow matter.

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Mineral scale builds on the heat exchanger and narrows its passages. The result is error codes, temperature swings and a shorter life. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit with isolation valves, and it is the single most useful maintenance task.

Most manufacturers call for a yearly flush, and heavier use or harder water may call for more. The City of Keizer publishes an annual water report for its groundwater system, and you can test your tap to learn more about your own water. Ask the manufacturer for its schedule.

The unit’s inlet screen and any filter also need cleaning. A clogged screen starves the unit of flow and triggers faults.

Filters, sediment screens and flow

An inlet filter protects the heat exchanger from debris. It needs to be cleaned or replaced on a schedule, and a clogged one can look like a failing heater. We show you how to check it and clean it between visits.

Flow rate is the other limit. A unit has a minimum flow to fire and a maximum it can heat. Low-flow fixtures can fall below the minimum, which makes hot water cut in and out.

Standards that govern tankless installs

A tankless install must meet the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), plus the manufacturer’s installation instructions, which set gas line size, venting material, clearances and water quality limits. Gas units follow the fuel gas code adopted for your area, and electric units are wired under the National Electrical Code (NEC).

Because the manufacturer’s manual carries real requirements, ask your plumber to show the venting and gas sizing sections for your exact model. A permit and an inspection are normal.

Safety checklist for a gas tankless heater

Gas appliances demand a few firm habits. Follow these steps.

  • ✓
