Toilet repair starts with where the water goes

Most toilet problems come down to three spots: the tank, the base and the drain. Water that runs in the tank is a flapper or fill valve problem. Water at the base is a seal problem. A bowl that will not empty is a drain problem. Telling us which one you have speeds the visit.

An overflowing toilet is the urgent one. Our 24/7 line answers at any hour and sends a plumber as soon as a crew is free. Until then, lift the tank lid, push the float up and close the shutoff valve at the wall.

When an overflowing toilet is an emergency

A toilet that is spilling over, or a bowl that rises when you flush, can ruin a floor in minutes. Close the valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and hold the flapper closed while you call.

Do not keep flushing, and do not use a plunger on a bowl that is full of sewage backing up from the line. That points to a deeper clog, not just the toilet.

  • ✓
    Close the supply valve. It is on the wall or floor behind the toilet. Turn clockwise.
  • ✓
    Stop flushing. Each flush adds water to the bowl.
  • ✓
    Check other drains. If a tub or sink is also slow, the clog is in the line, not the toilet. See drain cleaning.
  • ✓
    Mop and ventilate. Wipe up water quickly and open a window if there is any sewage smell.

The common toilet faults

Here is what each symptom usually means.

Running toilet

A worn flapper or a stuck fill valve lets water leak from the tank to the bowl. It wastes water all day.

Phantom flush

The toilet refills on its own because the flapper leaks slowly.

Leak at the base

A failed wax ring or loose closet bolts let water escape at the floor.

Weak flush

Clogged rim jets, a low tank level or a partial clog slow the flush.

Rocking toilet

A loose base or a damaged flange needs resetting and often a new ring.

Flanges, wax rings and the floor under the toilet

The flange is the ring on the floor that holds the toilet and connects it to the drain. A cracked or corroded flange lets the toilet rock and the seal fail. A repair ring can restore it if the floor is sound.

If water has been leaking at the base for a while, the subfloor can be soft. We check it before setting a new toilet, and tell you if wood needs replacing first. A leak that started inside the wall or under the floor needs leak detection before repair.

Keizer notes

Toilets in Keizer homes

Many Keizer homes were built in the 1960s and 1970s, and original toilets from that time may use more water per flush than a current one. A running toilet is the quiet culprit behind a high water bill, and a dye test in the tank takes only a few minutes to confirm it.

Homes in Woodburn and Salem draw from different water systems, but toilet parts are the same. Tell dispatch your address and we plan the visit with the right parts on the truck.
