What sewer line cleaning is

Sewer line cleaning removes the blockage and the buildup in the main line between your house and the public sewer. It uses a large cable machine with a cutter or a hydro jet, depending on what the line holds.

This is different from clearing a single drain. A fixture clog sits close to the sink or tub. A main line clog sits 20 to 100 feet away, and it takes a heavier machine. For fixtures see drain cleaning.

What blocks a main line

The cause drives the tool.

Roots

Fine roots enter through joints and grow into a net that catches paper.

Grease

Grease sticks to the pipe wall and narrows it over time.

Wipes and paper products

Products labeled flushable still tangle and clog.

Scale and debris

Older pipes shed rust and mineral flakes that collect at low spots.

A belly

A sag in the pipe holds water and solids and clogs repeatedly.

Keeping a sewer line clear

Do not flush wipes, paper towels or feminine products, even if the package says flushable. Keep grease out of the kitchen drain. Know where your cleanout is and keep the cap accessible.

If the line has backed up once, it will likely do it again unless the cause is addressed. A routine cleaning every year or two helps on lines with roots. A camera look tells you if cleaning is enough.

Guidelines that keep sewer cleaning safe for your pipes

Cable machines and jetters come with manufacturer guidelines for cable size, nozzle type and pressure. The right choice depends on the pipe material, the diameter and the blockage, so a quick look at the pipe type before cutting roots is standard practice.

The Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code expects accessible cleanouts, which are what make main line cleaning possible without breaking into the house. If yours is buried or missing, ask about adding one at the same visit.
