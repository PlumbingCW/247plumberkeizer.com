What repiping means

Repiping replaces the water supply pipes in a home, either the whole system or a major section. The new pipe is typically PEX or copper. It does not mean replacing drains, which are a separate system.

A repipe is a planned project. If a pipe has burst, burst pipe repair stops the damage first, and a repipe may follow if the rest of the system is failing.

Signs it is time to repipe

One leak is a repair. A pattern is a reason to replace.

Repeated leaks

A second or third leak in a short time on the same system means more are coming.

Rusty or brown water

Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and sheds rust.

Low water pressure

Corrosion narrows old pipes and chokes flow at the fixtures.

Pinhole leaks in copper

Small leaks in several places suggest the pipe wall is thinning.

Older polybutylene pipe

This gray plastic used in some homes in the late 1970s through the mid 1990s is known to fail.

PEX, copper or something else

The material changes cost, speed and feel.

PEXFlexible, fast to install, fewer joints, resists freeze splittingNeeds UV protection, fittings differ by system

Water service lines and pressure problems

The service line runs from the meter to the house. If it leaks or corrodes, pressure falls and water use rises. A service line is a separate project from the interior repipe, and we look at both together when pressure is the symptom.

Low pressure has other causes too, such as a failing pressure regulator or a partly closed valve. We test pressure at the hose bib first, so we repair the right thing. If a meter runs with the house off, start with leak detection.

Permits and planning

The City of Keizer lists plumbing among the work that needs a permit for alterations to existing buildings, and permits are issued to properly licensed and bonded contractors. The Building Department is at 930 Chemawa Road NE and the number is (503) 856-3440. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection for the project.

Ask us about timing. A whole-house repipe usually means a day or two without normal water use, and we plan the sequence so the water is back on each evening when possible.

Codes and materials for a whole-house repipe

A repipe is regulated work. The materials, joints, support spacing and water pressure limits come from the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and a permit with inspections is the normal path. Pipe makers also publish installation instructions that must be followed for the warranty to mean anything.

Ask your plumber which pipe material will be used and why, who pulls the permit and when inspections happen. Verify the license through the Oregon Building Codes Division license search before you sign.
