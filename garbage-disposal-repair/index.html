What goes wrong with disposals

A disposal has a motor, a grinding plate and a drain connection. Most calls come from one of those three. The motor jams on something hard, the plate wears dull or the seals and connections leak.

A hum with no spin means the plate is jammed. A silent unit usually has a tripped overload button underneath. A leak from the top flange, the side drain or the bottom of the housing needs different repairs, and we tell them apart quickly.

Symptoms and their usual causes

Match what you see to what is likely.

Humming, no spin

A jam. A hex wrench in the bottom socket often frees it.

Nothing at all

A tripped reset button or a dead outlet.

Leak at the top

A worn sink flange seal or loose mounting.

Leak at the bottom

A cracked housing or failed seal. This usually means replacement.

Slow sink drain

A clog past the disposal. See drain cleaning.

Installing a new disposal

A new disposal fits the existing sink opening. We remove the old unit, set the mounting, connect the drain and the dishwasher line, and wire the plug or switch. Horsepower and feed type are picked to match your kitchen use.

Not every home needs a disposal. Many homes in Keizer already have one, and for them we match the model. If your home has none, we install a new one at the same visit.

Keizer notes

Disposals in Keizer kitchens

Kitchen drains from the 1960s and 1970s can be narrower and rougher than new ones, and grease from a disposal can build up fast in them. Scrape plates, run cold water while grinding and avoid pouring grease down the sink. A disposal that works but a sink that is slow points to the line, not the disposal.

Customers in Salem and Woodburn should follow the same habit, since grease clogs lines in every city.

Electrical and safety rules for disposals

A garbage disposal is wired under the National Electrical Code (NEC), either hard-wired or on a cord, depending on the unit and the manufacturer’s instructions. Switch the power off at the breaker before any work, because a wall switch alone is not enough.

Do not put your hand in the chamber, ever. Use the hex wrench socket under the unit to free a jam, or a wooden spoon handle from above. Any wiring change beyond a cord swap belongs with a licensed electrician.
