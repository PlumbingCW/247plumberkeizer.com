How to verify any plumber in Oregon

The Oregon Building Codes Division licenses plumbers and publishes a license search on its website. The Construction Contractors Board has its own search, and a plumber must hold a Construction Contractors Board license or work for a licensed business.

A few minutes online before work starts protects you. If you suspect unlicensed plumbing, the state asks you to document it with photos, business names and invoices, and to report it.

What we do not do

We do not drill wells, repair well pumps, service septic tanks or install fire sprinklers. We do not give legal or insurance advice. We can tell you what is usually covered, but your policy decides, and your insurer is the one to ask.

How pricing works

We do not publish flat prices on the website, because plumbing problems differ from house to house. What we promise is a clear price before work starts, with the factors explained in plain words. Each service page lists the factors that move the number, so you know what to expect.

Nights, weekends and holidays carry an after-hours rate. Dispatch tells you the rate on the call, before anyone is sent. If the price does not suit you, you can decline and owe nothing for the work.

Why the website has guides

Plumbing problems are stressful, and a quick explanation helps. The blog covers questions Keizer homeowners ask, from pipe lining to water heater costs, with local notes drawn from city and state sources. The guides are general information, and your house may differ.

Where the local facts on this site come from

Every local fact on these pages comes from a public source such as a city website, a utility water report, a state licensing page or a county permit page. When a source does not give a number, we leave it out. For example, the City of Keizer publishes an annual water report, but the page we reviewed does not state a hardness figure, so none appears here.

Local facts change. Permit offices move, utilities update their reports and codes are revised. Treat the contacts on this site as a starting point and confirm with the named office before you rely on them. If you spot something that looks out of date, email contact@247plumberkeizer.com and we will check it.

We also avoid claims we cannot back. You will not find invented customer counts, star ratings, testimonials or license numbers on this site. A plumber is licensed or is not, and the state license search is the place to verify it. We would rather leave a gap than fill it with a guess.
