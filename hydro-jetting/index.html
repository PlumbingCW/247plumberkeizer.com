What hydro jetting is

Hydro jetting pushes water through a hose and a steel nozzle at high pressure. The nozzle sends jets forward to break up a blockage and backward to pull itself along and scour the wall. The water carries the debris out through the cleanout.

Where a cable makes a hole in the clog, a jet restores the full diameter of the pipe. That is why it is the better fix for grease buildup, scale and root masses, and for any line that clogs again soon after cabling.

What jetting is best at

These are the situations where we reach for the jetter first.

Grease in kitchen and restaurant lines

Hot grease cools into a hard layer that cables only punch through.

Tree roots

Roots enter at joints and grow into fibrous mats. Jetting clears them back toward the pipe wall.

Scale and sludge

Mineral and soap buildup narrows the pipe over years and jets break it off.

Before and after relining

A clean pipe is needed before a liner goes in, so jetting prepares the line.

When jetting is the wrong tool

High pressure water is not for every pipe. A cracked, collapsed or severely corroded line can be damaged by jetting, and a camera look first tells us if the pipe can take it. In those cases, sewer line repair is the correct next step.

Cast iron with heavy scale can shed in large flakes, so we work the line in stages. Thin or fragile pipe gets a lower pressure setting. If we judge the pipe too fragile to jet, we say so and explain why.

Pressure limits and guidelines for jetting

Jetting equipment is built for specific pipe conditions, and manufacturer guidelines set nozzle choice and pressure limits for each pipe material. Scaled cast iron, clay with root-damaged joints and PVC do not tolerate the same settings.

That is why a camera look before jetting is standard practice. It confirms the pipe can take the pressure. Jetting cleans a line, it does not repair one, and any repair that follows is regulated work under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
