Silverton homes, built in layers

Silverton was platted in 1854 along Silver Creek, and the Commercial Historic District still anchors downtown. Homes within a few blocks of North Water Street and Town Square Park span more than a century of building. The plumbing in them spans just as many materials, from clay and cast iron drains to galvanized steel and later copper.

That mix is the starting point for any call here. A plumber who knows the age of the house knows which pipe to expect, and which one is likely to fail first. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, and the line is the same one that serves Keizer.

What the age of the house tells us

Match your home to the usual story.

Pre-war homes near downtown

Mid-century homes

Copper supply lines, cast iron or early plastic drains. Check joints and old shutoffs.

Newer subdivisions

PEX supply lines and plastic drains. Failures are usually fittings or fixtures.

Rural lots outside town

How Silverton’s water arrives at your tap

Most of the City of Silverton’s water comes from Abiqua Creek. A Silver Creek intake can also supply water, and a reservoir holds about 1,300 acre-feet, roughly 420 million gallons, as a buffer. The treatment plant sits at the corner of Ames and East Main Streets.

The city’s 2023 consumer confidence report found no detectable lead or copper in its testing, and it suggests flushing a tap for 30 seconds to two minutes if water has sat for hours. That tip is worth following after a long vacation, and again after we replace a section of pipe, since fresh pipe can shed debris.

The report does not give a hardness figure. Test your tap, or ask the city for current information, before you buy a softener.

Keizer notes

Landmarks and what they mean for your plumbing

Silverton sits at the eastern edge of the Willamette Valley, with Silver Falls State Park and the 130-acre Oregon Garden nearby. Any home that sits empty for weeks, such as a second home or a rental between tenants, often reveals a dripping valve or a tired heater when the water comes back on.

Along Silver Creek, older buildings on North Water Street sit close to the water, which makes sewer laterals and storm drains worth a camera inspection. Mount Angel is adjacent, and Woodburn is about 12 miles away on Oregon Route 214.

Rural properties around Silverton

The Silverton ZIP, 97381, covers more than 110 square miles, much of it rural. Properties outside the city may use a private well or a septic tank. Tell dispatch which applies. A well-fed home has a pressure tank and a pump, and a septic home needs different drain service.

We handle plumbing inside the house on rural properties. For pump, well and septic tank work, we tell you if the job is outside our service and point you to the right trade.

Code and licensing rules for plumbing in Silverton

Plumbing in Silverton follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The City of Silverton Building Department handles plumbing permits, and its application is online, so the city is the first call for a water heater, repipe or drain change.

You can check any plumber’s license through the Oregon Building Codes Division license search before you hire.
