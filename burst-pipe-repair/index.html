Burst pipe: what to do first

A burst pipe floods a home in minutes. Our 24/7 dispatch answers at any hour and sends a plumber as soon as a crew is free. The first job is yours: close the main water shutoff, usually near the water meter, where the line enters the house or in the garage.

Once the water is off, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines, switch off power to wet rooms only if the breaker is dry and reachable, and call us.

  • ✓

Why pipes crack or burst

A burst is the end of a slower story. These are the usual causes.

Freezing

Water expands as it freezes and splits pipes in unheated spaces such as crawlspaces and exterior walls.

Corrosion

Old galvanized steel rusts from the inside, thinning the wall until it gives way.

High pressure

Pressure above normal strains fittings, and a failing regulator can cause bursts.

Movement

Shifting soil and settling can crack drain and supply lines.

Faulty fittings

A bad coupling or supply line fails suddenly, often without warning.

How we repair a burst pipe

The repair depends on the pipe and the break. A split in copper or PEX is cut out and replaced with a new section and proper fittings. A failed galvanized section is replaced with modern pipe, since patching old steel often just moves the leak to the next weak spot.

If more than one section has failed, repiping becomes the better option. See repiping for whole-house replacement. When the water source is hidden, leak detection finds the break first.

The material in the wall changes the repair.

Galvanized steelInternal rust and thinning wallsReplace the section and consider repiping

These are common patterns, not a diagnosis. The plumber confirms the pipe material on site.

Frozen pipes and other seasonal risks

When pipes in an unheated space freeze, you may see no water at a faucet before you see a burst. Turn off the main valve before you try thawing, and never use an open flame. If a pipe thaws and splits, you want the water already off.

Insulate exposed pipes in crawlspaces and garages, open cabinet doors on exterior walls during hard freezes and know where your main valve is before you need it.

Materials, code and safety after a pipe bursts

Replacement pipe and fittings for drinking water must be materials that the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), as adopted in Oregon, approves for potable use. Joints should be made the way the pipe maker specifies, because a bad fitting is a common cause of a second failure.

Safety comes first while you wait. Do not stand in water near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel. If water has reached any of them, leave the area and have power shut off by a professional. Close the main water valve, open the lowest tap to drain the lines and photograph the damage for your insurer.
