What plumbing installation covers

Plumbing installation is the planned work of adding or moving plumbing, as opposed to repairing it. That includes new sinks, toilets, showers and tubs, laundry hookups, water lines to a new room and drain changes for a remodel.

It also includes rough-in work in a new addition, where pipes go in before the walls close. We coordinate with your contractor so the plumbing is in place and inspected before drywall.

Common installation projects

These are the jobs we are asked about most.

Kitchen remodels

Moving a sink, adding a dishwasher line or a pot filler, and installing a disposal.

Bathroom remodels

New vanity, tub or shower valve, relocated toilet or an added bath.

Laundry hookups

Washer box, drain and shutoff for a new laundry area.

Water lines and hose bibs

New outdoor spigots, or lines to an outbuilding or garage.

Fixture swaps

Replacing a sink, faucet or toilet with a new model.

Permits and licensed work

New plumbing usually needs a permit. The City of Keizer says permits are required for new construction and alterations and additions, and are issued to properly licensed and bonded contractors. The Building Department is at 930 Chemawa Road NE and the phone is (503) 856-3440.

Oregon requires plumbers to be licensed by the Building Codes Division, and the Construction Contractors Board also requires a license. You can check both online before you hire anyone. We follow the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code for all new work. If you are also replacing a heater, see water heater installation.

Keizer notes

Planning a project in Keizer

Keizer homes were mostly built between the 1950s and 1970s, and many have been remodeled since. A kitchen or bath remodel often uncovers old supply lines, so build room into the schedule. If the old lines are galvanized, repiping at the same time can save a second opening of the walls.

Projects in Silverton go through the Silverton building department, and the city lists online applications for plumbing. Woodburn works differently, as Marion County issues plumbing permits there, so we confirm the right office for your address.

Code points behind new plumbing work

New fixtures, drains and supply lines must meet the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The code sizes pipes by fixture units, requires traps and vents on drains and lists approved materials for water and waste lines.

A permit and an inspection are normal for new work, and the inspector checks what will be hidden behind walls before they close. Ask your plumber to name the inspection steps and the pipe materials before work begins.
