What commercial plumbing covers

Commercial plumbing handles larger fixtures, longer pipe runs and heavier use than a home. That includes restrooms, kitchens, break rooms, water heaters, floor drains, grease lines and backflow assemblies.

We serve small shops, offices, restaurants, retail centers and multi-unit buildings around Keizer. We do not handle large industrial processes or fire sprinkler work. If a job is outside our scope, we say so and point you elsewhere.

Common business calls

These are the problems that bring in the most calls.

Restroom stoppages

High-use toilets and urinals clog from paper products and wipes.

Kitchen drain backups

Grease and food waste clog floor drains and sinks. See hydro jetting.

Water heater failure

Commercial heaters run longer hours and fail on a different schedule. See water heater repair.

Leaks and low pressure

Hidden leaks drive up water bills. See leak detection.

Grease traps, storm drains and backflow

Grease traps and interceptors catch fats before they reach the sewer. They need regular pumping and cleaning, and a full trap causes backups and odors. We service small and mid-size traps and advise on a schedule.

Storm drains and catch basins clog with leaves, silt and trash. A parking lot that floods in rain is often a clogged inlet. Backflow assemblies protect the drinking water supply from contamination. Ask your water provider how often yours must be tested, since schedules vary.

An honest note on commercial leak detection

Leak detection in large commercial buildings is harder than in a home. Long runs, multiple tenants and shared systems make it difficult to find a leak quickly. We do it for small and mid-size properties, and we tell you if a building is too big or complex for our tools.

Code and permits for commercial plumbing

Commercial plumbing in Oregon is governed by the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the work generally needs permits and inspections through the local building authority. Restaurants, offices and shops each have fixture counts, drainage and venting needs that the code sizes by fixture units.

Before you hire, ask for the permit plan, the inspection steps and how the plumber will keep your water on during business hours. Verify the license through the Oregon Building Codes Division license search.
