Emergency plumber in Keizer: your first ten minutes

Most plumbing emergencies are made worse in the first ten minutes by water that keeps running. Find your main shutoff now, before you need it. It is usually near the water meter, where the line enters the house or in the garage. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Then open the lowest tap to drain the lines and call the 24/7 line. If sewage is coming up, stop all water use and do not flush. If a water heater is leaking, close its cold valve and turn off the power or gas.

Keep your phone charged and your street number ready. Dispatch asks for the cross street, because Keizer has many roads off River Road North and Chemawa Road with similar names.

What counts as an emergency and what can wait

Call an emergency plumber for active water you cannot stop, sewage in the house, no hot water in cold weather, a leaking water heater tank or a toilet that will not stop overflowing. These get a same-day response when a crew is free, day or night.

A dripping faucet, a slow drain in one sink or a running toilet can wait until daytime, though the 24/7 line can book those too. If you are unsure, call and describe it. Dispatch tells you whether it is safe to wait.

For planned jobs, such as water heater installation, repiping or plumbing installation, we schedule a day that fits and give a clear price first.

Keizer’s plumbing, built in layers

The City of Keizer was incorporated on November 2, 1982, when Clear Lake joined it, but its houses are older than the city. Development took off in the 1950s after dams stabilized the Willamette River, and through the 1970s orchards and berry fields turned into subdivisions. Homes built in those decades are now 50 to 70 years old.

That age shows up in the pipes. Galvanized steel supply lines corrode from the inside, cast iron drains scale and crack, and roots find old joints. A sewer camera inspection or a leak detection visit shows what is going on before you spend on a repair.

The city supplies water from the Troutdale aquifer through 15 deep wells and about 125 miles of pipe, and it publishes an annual water report. Ask for it if you want to know what is in your supply, and test your tap if you are shopping for a softener.

Licensing, permits and how to check a plumber

Oregon requires plumbers to be licensed by the Building Codes Division, and plumbing contractors must also hold a Construction Contractors Board license or work for a licensed business. You can check both online in a few minutes, and you should check anyone you hire.

The City of Keizer Building Department is at 930 Chemawa Road NE, phone (503) 856-3440, and the city says permits are required for alterations and additions to existing buildings. Permits are issued to properly licensed and bonded contractors, so ask the department whether your job needs one.

Codes and licensing behind plumbing work in the Keizer area

Plumbing in Oregon follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Permits and inspections are issued by the local building office for your address, which is the City of Keizer for Keizer homes.

Before you hire anyone, check the plumber’s license with the Oregon Building Codes Division license search. It takes a few minutes, and it is the one credential you can verify yourself.
