What a sewer camera inspection is

A sewer inspection sends a waterproof camera through your line from a cleanout or a vent. It records video and shows the position of what it sees along the pipe. The result is a clear picture of the line instead of a guess.

It is the safest first step before repair. Blind digging costs more than any camera visit, and a camera look often shows a cheaper fix. If the line is simply clogged, see sewer line cleaning.

When to book an inspection

These situations call for a look inside.

Buying a home

The sewer lateral is out of sight. A camera look before closing can reveal costly damage.

Repeat backups

A line that keeps clogging after cleaning has a cause the camera can find.

Before landscaping or digging

Know where the line runs and its condition before heavy work.

After roots or a nearby tree removal

See if roots have entered joints or cracked the pipe.

How inspection findings are described and reported

Many municipal and professional pipeline inspectors code defects using the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program system, which is a recognized way to name cracks, roots, offset joints and sags. When you buy a report, ask whether it uses a recognized coding method and ask for the recording itself.

Repairs that follow an inspection fall under the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is the state’s adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so a defect noted on camera usually becomes a permitted repair if you go ahead. Keep the report. It is useful in a sale negotiation and for planning.
