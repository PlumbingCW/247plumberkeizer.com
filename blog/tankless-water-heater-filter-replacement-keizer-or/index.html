Tankless Water Heater Filter Replacement in Keizer

The small screen on a tankless heater protects the heat exchanger. When it clogs, hot water cuts out. Here is how to check it.

By the Keizer 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

What the filter does

A tankless water heater has a small inlet screen or filter, usually where the cold water enters the unit. It stops sand, scale flakes and bits of pipe debris before they reach the heat exchanger. Without it, those particles could block narrow passages.

Because it catches debris, it clogs. A clogged filter restricts flow. Tankless units need a minimum flow to fire, so a restricted inlet can make the heater shut off or fail to light.

Signs the filter is clogged

Look for these symptoms before you call.

Weak hot water flow

Cold flow is fine, but hot comes out slowly.

Heater cycles on and off

Low flow drops below the unit’s minimum and it shuts down.

Error code about flow or ignition

Many brands show a code tied to water flow or temperature.

Hot water gets worse over weeks

Gradual buildup of debris is the usual story.

How to check and clean it

Check the manual for your model first. This is the usual sequence.

1

Shut off water and power

Close the isolation valves, turn off gas or power, and relieve pressure at a hot tap.

2

Remove and inspect

Unscrew the inlet filter, check for debris and rinse it under running water.

3

Reinstall and test

Put it back with its washer, open the valves slowly, purge air and test hot water at several taps.

Filter, descale or call a plumber

The right action depends on what you find.

FindingActionWho
Debris on the screenRinse and reinstallHomeowner
Torn or damaged screenReplace the filterHomeowner or plumber
Screen clean but flow weakDescale the heat exchangerPlumber, see water heater repair
Leak at the filter housingReplace the washer or housingPlumber

Do not run a tankless unit without the filter in place.

How often to check

Check the filter at least once a year with your flush, and sooner if you notice any of the symptoms above. After plumbing work in the house, check it again. New pipe, a repaired main or a water shutoff can stir debris loose.

If the filter is always full, the debris source matters. It can be old galvanized pipe, a failing water heater anode in a tank upstream or work on the supply. A plumber can trace it.

Why it ties into the yearly flush

The filter protects the unit from debris. The flush protects it from scale. They are different tasks done at the same time. Use isolation valves and a descaling solution as the manual describes, and clean the filter after the flush. For more on service, see tankless water heater care.

Why debris shows up after plumbing work

A repair, a new fixture or a shutoff of the main can stir up debris that has been sitting quietly in the pipes. That material heads for the first screen it meets, and on many homes that is the tankless inlet filter.

If your hot water weakens a day or two after plumbing work, the filter is the first thing to check. It is a five minute task, and it often brings the heater back to normal without a service call.

Pair the filter check with the other upkeep a tankless unit needs.

When the filter is not the culprit

If you clean the screen and the problem remains, the next suspects are scale on the heat exchanger and a failing flow sensor. Scale narrows the passages inside the unit and causes temperature swings. A flow sensor that reads wrong makes the heater think the flow is too low to fire.

Both are service jobs. A descale uses a pump and solution through the isolation valves. A sensor replacement is a part swap. Neither is a reason to replace a unit that is otherwise sound, so ask for a diagnosis before accepting a replacement quote. Water heater repair visits cover this kind of work.

A few details speed up the diagnosis.

The brand and model

The label on the unit shows both.

The error code, if any

Codes point to the likely fault.

Right after plumbing work, or gradually?

Following the manufacturer on filters and flushing

The manufacturer’s manual sets the filter location, the cleaning interval and the flush procedure for your model, and the warranty often depends on following it. Treat the manual as the authority, since its installation guidelines are written for your exact unit.

Turn off the power or gas and the water supply before opening any filter. Gas units should also be checked for odors, and a rotten egg smell means leave and call the gas utility.

Choosing a replacement filter

Replacement filters are specific to each brand and often to each model. A screen that looks the same can have a different mesh size or seal. Use the part number in the manual or on the old filter, and order from the manufacturer or a trusted supplier.

Keep one spare on hand. A filter is small and inexpensive, and having a spare turns a possible service call into a five minute swap. Store it in a clean bag so the screen stays free of dust and lint.

If you use a whole house sediment filter ahead of the heater, check that one too. A clogged upstream filter looks like a failing heater, and the fix is a cartridge change. Write the dates of both checks on a label near the heater so you remember them.
