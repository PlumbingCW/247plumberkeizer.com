Leaking Garbage Disposal Repair for Keizer Homes

Water under the sink does not always come from the disposal. Trace it by location before you decide to repair or replace.

By the Keizer 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 13, 2026

First, find the real source

A wet cabinet under the sink has several possible sources: the faucet, the supply lines, the dishwasher hose, the drain trap and the disposal. Dry everything with a towel, then run water slowly and watch where the first drop appears.

Putting a dry paper towel at each joint is the fastest way to catch a slow leak. The leak that appears first is the one to fix first, and often it is not the disposal at all.

Where the disposal leaks and what it means

The location points to the cause.

Leak locationLikely causeRepair or replace
Top, where it meets the sinkWorn flange seal or loose mountRepair: reseal or tighten
Side, at the drain pipeLoose or worn drain gasketRepair: new gasket
Dishwasher inletLoose hose clampRepair: new clamp
Bottom of the housingCracked housing or failed sealReplace
Reset button areaInternal leakReplace

A leak at the bottom almost always means the unit is done.

Checking for a repairable leak

These steps are safe if the power is off.

1

Cut power

Unplug the unit or turn off its breaker. Never put a hand inside.

2

Tighten and test

Snug the mounting ring and the drain connection, then run water and check.

3

Replace the gasket if needed

A dripping drain gasket is a cheap part that a plumber can swap quickly.

Why disposals leak at the top

The sink flange sits in the drain hole with a seal. Over time, plumber’s putty or the gasket dries out and water seeps past it. The leak shows up as drips down the side of the unit. Resealing the flange fixes it, though it means removing the disposal.

A disposal that leaks only when the dishwasher runs usually has a loose or damaged dishwasher inlet. See garbage disposal repair for the full list of faults.

When to replace and what to expect

A leak through the housing is the end. A new disposal is a same-visit job for a plumber. Match the horsepower to your kitchen. A larger motor grinds more and runs quieter.

Avoid grease, bones, fibrous scraps and coffee grounds. Even a new unit can clog the drain if grease goes down. A slow sink with a working disposal points to the line, and drain cleaning handles that.

Cost drivers

A gasket or clamp is a low-cost repair. A reseal costs more because the unit comes off. A replacement depends on the model. After-hours visits carry a rate that dispatch quotes first.

Quick triage: the dishwasher and the trap

Before blaming the disposal, run the dishwasher by itself and watch the connection at the disposal inlet. A loose hose clamp is an easy fix. Then run the sink with the stopper in and the disposal off, and check the trap under the cabinet for drips at each nut.

Working through the parts one at a time matters because a single wet spot can drip down onto a lower part and fool you. Dry each connection and test it alone.

Habits that prevent the next leak

A disposal lasts longer when it is used gently.

  • ✓
    Run cold water while grindingIt helps solids move through and keeps grease firmer.
  • ✓
    Skip bones and fibrous scrapsCelery, corn husks and potato peels wrap around the grinding parts.
  • ✓
    Do not pour grease inIt cools and coats the drain beyond the disposal.
  • ✓
    Check under the sink monthlyA quick look catches a weep before it ruins the cabinet.

Is it worth repairing an old disposal?

A disposal is a simple machine, but it lives a hard life. If the unit is many years old and starts to leak, the seals that failed are probably not the only worn parts. The bearings, the grinding plate and the motor have been working just as long.

A cheap gasket on a young unit is worth the repair. A reseal on an old unit that is also loud, slow and prone to jams often buys only a short reprieve. Ask the plumber for an honest opinion, and compare the repair cost with the cost of a basic new unit.

Choosing a replacement

If it is time for a new unit, these features matter.

Horsepower

More power grinds tougher scraps and runs more quietly.

Feed type

Continuous feed units run while the switch is on. Batch feed units run with the stopper in place.

Mounting style

Match the existing sink mount so the swap is easy.

Noise insulation

Insulated housings are quieter in an open kitchen.

Safety and electrical rules for disposals

A disposal is wired under the National Electrical Code (NEC) and comes with manufacturer instructions for mounting, sealing and drain connections. Switch the power off at the breaker before you touch it.

Never put a hand in the chamber. Leaks at the flange, hoses or housing each have a different repair, and the manufacturer’s manual shows which parts are serviceable.

What to do right now if it is leaking

Switch the disposal off at the wall and unplug it if you can reach the cord. Empty the cabinet and place a bowl or a bucket under the unit. If the leak is heavy, close the shutoff valves under the sink. They are the small oval valves on the supply pipes.

Take a photo of the leak and the label on the disposal. The label shows the brand, the model and often the date, and it helps a plumber bring the right gasket or a matching replacement. Dry the cabinet and the floor under it so you can tell whether the leak is still going.

Do not use the sink until the plumber has looked at it, especially if the leak is at the bottom of the unit. Water pouring through the housing can reach the motor and the wiring, and the combination of water and electricity is the real danger in this job.
